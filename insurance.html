What the application will ask

Wording varies by insurer, but most small-business cyber applications come back to the same controls. These are the ones that most often decide the price, or whether a policy is offered at all.

Multi-factor authentication

On email, remote access and every administrator account, including your IT provider’s.

Backups ransomware can’t reach

At least one offline or immutable copy, and a restore actually tested in the last year.

Endpoint protection

Endpoint detection and response on every computer and server, not just some of them.

Remote access closed to the internet

No remote desktop or admin ports open to the world.

Payment verification

A call-back rule before anyone sends money or changes bank details.

Patching and email security

Critical updates applied promptly, and email filtering with SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

A plan for the bad day

A written incident response plan with your insurer’s hotline in it, tested at least once a year.

Trained staff

Annual security training and phishing tests, with records to show for them.

What you get

Insurers increasingly check answers against evidence and against what they can see of you from the internet. Everything here is built so each “yes” on your application can be backed up.

  1. The question map. Every question underwriters ask, answered for your business. A “yes” counts only when there is dated proof behind it; anything else is marked “yes, not proven” until it is.
  2. Coverage counts, not impressions. “MFA on admin accounts” becomes “12 of 12 admin accounts”, and “EDR everywhere” is checked against your device list, so partial coverage shows up before the insurer finds it.
  3. An outside-in check. We look at your business the way insurers’ scanners do: email protection records (SPF and DMARC), exposed remote access, certificates and known breaches, and reconcile it with your answers.
  4. An evidence request for your IT provider. A dated list of exactly which exports and screenshots to send, so the proof comes from the systems themselves.
  5. An evidence binder. The proof for each answer, dated and filed, ready if an underwriter or a claims adjuster asks. Evidence older than 90 days is flagged for a refresh.
  6. A fix-before-you-apply plan. Gaps and unproven answers ranked by how much they matter to an insurer, each with an owner and a date before your renewal.
  7. A controls summary for your broker. One or two pages your broker can use to place your coverage, listing only what is proven, confirmed by you. An evidence schedule is added at the end: it indexes your proof by reference number without sending the sensitive files themselves, so you can produce any item if asked.

What this is, and isn’t

I don’t sell insurance and I can’t promise coverage, terms or a price; your insurer decides. What I do is help you answer the application truthfully and well. A wrong answer can give an insurer grounds to deny a claim later, so we close gaps rather than paper over them, and we keep the proof. Evidence shows a control was in place on the date it was collected; keeping it current is part of the renewal refresh. I guide; your team or IT provider makes the changes.
