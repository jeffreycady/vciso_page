A real case · my own family

The fake job offer

What happened

My son was "hired" for a job that didn't exist. The offer came from a real company's HR email account, which criminals had broken into, and the onboarding happened over Microsoft Teams. As part of the onboarding, they sent him a fraudulent check. If it can happen in a security professional's family, it can happen in anyone's.

What we did

  • Reported the fraudulent check to his bank
  • Filed a report with the FBI
  • Told the HR person whose email account had been taken over, so the company could lock the criminals out
  • Reported the Teams account used in the scam to Microsoft, which shut it down

Why it matters

Recovery isn't only about cleaning one computer. It's knowing who to call, in what order, and what to say, so the scam stops and the next person isn't caught by it.

The fake bank email

What happens

An email arrives that looks like it's from the bank. The link leads to a copy of the bank's website. Once the username and password are typed in, the scammer has them and starts moving money.

What we'd do

  • Call the bank's fraud department together and report the transfers as unauthorized
  • Secure email, banking and investment accounts, starting with email
  • Turn on a second login step and set up a password manager
  • Write up a clear timeline for the bank's dispute process

What you'd end up with

A documented claim with the bank, accounts the scammer can no longer reach, and a routine for telling real bank messages from fake ones.

The fake tech support pop-up

What happens

A loud warning fills the screen saying the computer is infected and to call a number. The person who answers asks for remote access, installs harmful software, and then charges hundreds of dollars to "fix" it.

What we'd do

  • Back up photos and documents, then wipe the computer and reinstall it cleanly
  • Dispute the charge with the card company
  • Change passwords for anything used on that computer
  • Show you and a family member how to spot and close fake warnings

What you'd end up with

A clean computer, new passwords, and the confidence to close the next pop-up without calling the number.

Should I go to the police?

It depends on what happened, and we'll talk it through on the first call. In general, tell your bank first. You can also report it to:

  • The Federal Trade Commission at reportfraud.ftc.gov
  • The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov
  • Your local police or the Maricopa County Sheriff's Office, especially if you lost money

A report helps with bank disputes even when the police can't recover the money.
